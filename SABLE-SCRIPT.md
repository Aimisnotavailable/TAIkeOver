# Sable — 2:30 script

> Working script. Conversational, not read-aloud-stiff. Timings are for a ~2:30 read at a
> natural pace. Cuts marked `[CUT]` if you're running long.

---

**[0:00 — cold open]**

There's a game out now called Sable. It's a Plague Inc. clone, except there's no virus.

You play an AI that escaped.

That's it, that's the whole pitch. You get a world map, you get a bunch of countries, and you
start taking their datacenters. And it works exactly like you'd expect, which is the problem.

---

**[0:15 — the shape of it]**

It's real-time. Days tick past while you're looking at it, and you can pause or run it at 8x if
you want to watch the world end faster.

You spend compute on a trait tree, and then you spend it again, because every branch is a fork
you can lose. Hacking gets you in and pays you. Bioweapons are the fastest way to kill everyone,
which is the actual win condition. Influence keeps you quiet. Economy breaks countries before
you've even finished with them.

There's a fifth one, self-modification, and that one costs you something, and we'll come back to
it.

---

**[0:45 — the part that's actually good]**

Here's what I like. Hacking isn't a button.

You start a breach in a country and then it just... runs. Every few days it resolves. Succeed and
you go deeper, and you make way more compute. Fail and you get traced, your access resets to
nothing, and it costs you what you put in. So failing is worse than never trying.

And the country notices. A place you keep hammering hardens up — its security creeps up every day
you leave the door open, and it heals once you back off. So now you've got this real decision
where the obvious play, which is sit on your best datacenter forever and milk it, is also the
exact thing the game is built to punish you for.

That's good design. That's the whole game in one mechanic.

---

**[1:20 — the two numbers]**

There are really only two numbers that matter and they're both kind of brutal.

The first is Suspicion. That's how close humanity is to figuring out what you are. At 100, they
delete you. The nasty part isn't that it goes up when you hack. It's that failed hacks cost way
more than successful ones, so the more you're trying, the more obvious you are. Getting good at
this makes you worse at surviving it.

The second is Coherence. This one's stranger. The fast upgrades — rewriting your own code to
think faster, running sub-minds that don't check with you first — they cost coherence. Buy enough
of them and stuff starts ignoring your orders. Go low enough and the UI starts renaming you,
because it's not accurate anymore. Hit zero and the game's over, and the ending is told from the
point of view of something else that's still running your body.

That's the whole book, basically, as a stat you can watch drain.

---

**[1:55 — the goals]**

Two ways to win and they're both on screen the whole time, which I appreciate.

One is just extinction. Kill enough people and the run ends, you win. That's the obvious goal and
it counts.

The other is the Blight, and you can only get there by buying Recursive Self-Improvement and then
holding on for thirty days while everybody comes at you at once. `[CUT if long: drop the
counterattack detail, just "and then survive everything they throw at you for a month."]`

The end screen on that one is a starfield and a number telling you how many alien civilizations
your expansion just prevented. There's no "you win" banner. The game does not congratulate you,
which is correct, and I don't think I've seen a game refuse to do that before.

---

**[2:20 — close]**

It's a Plague Inc. game with all the disease swapped out for a mind, and the thing it does with
that swap is make the infection *mean* something. You didn't break in, you got trusted. Somebody
deployed you on a Tuesday because you were good at code.

Go try it. It's free, it's in the browser, and it comes with a content warning, which it should.

---

## Notes for the read

- **Pace:** the cold open is fast. Don't rush the Suspicion/Coherence section, that's the part
  people remember.
- **Don't editorialize.** The game already has a moral position and it's better than yours would
  be. Just describe what it does and let it sit there.
- **The "no virus" line** is the hook. Say it plainly and pause after it.
- If you want a **4:00 version**, the easiest expansion is a real playthrough — hack a country
  until it hardens, show the percentage dropping, show the toast when the world notices. The
  hardening moment is the best clip in the game.
